# Constitution of the County Confederation

**Preamble**: We, the people of the several counties, in order to secure liberty, preserve peace, and coordinate defense, do establish this Confederation. All powers not expressly delegated herein are reserved to the counties and their people.

---

## Article I: The County (Sovereign Unit)

### Section 1: County Sovereignty

Each county is a sovereign political community. Its authority derives from the consent of its resident citizens, expressed through its charter, ratified by majority vote of registered residents, and re-ratified every 20 years.

### Section 2: County Powers (Exclusive)

Each county exercises **all legislative, executive, and judicial powers** within its territory, except those expressly delegated to the Confederation or prohibited by this Constitution. Including but not limited to:

- Taxation (LVT, consumption, user fees)
- Police, courts, jails, militia
- Land use, zoning, building codes
- Health, welfare, education
- Currency acceptance, banking charter
- Corporate charter, licensing, contracts
- Environmental regulation
- Elections, recall, sortition
- Immigration, citizenship, entry (subject to the Art VI §2.13 asylum & non-refoulement limit)

### Section 3: County Charter

Every county adopts a written charter specifying:

- Government structure (commission, sheriff, judges, clerk, etc.)
- Term limits, recall thresholds, sortition procedures (county charters are sovereign on term limits; no constitutional maximum for county offices)
- Revenue sources, spending priorities, debt limits
- Mutual aid charters (pension, health, unemployment)
- Amendment process (resident referendum, 60% threshold)
- Secession clause (Art IV §3 invocation)

### Section 4: County Boundaries

County boundaries may be adjusted by mutual consent of affected county commissions + majority referendum in each affected county. New counties may be formed by petition of 10% of residents in a contiguous area + majority referendum. Counties may secede from a state by 60% referendum + consent of the receiving state's Council of Counties.

### Section 5: Corporate Personhood Revoked

Artificial entities (corporations, LLCs, partnerships, trusts) hold **no constitutional rights**. They possess only statutory privileges granted by their chartering county, revocable at will. No corporate claim under Article VI. Shareholders personally liable for corporate torts/crimes unless adequate insurance is carried (county risk board sets minimums).

## Article II: The State (Coordination + Minimum Standards)

### Section 1: State Composition

A state is a voluntary association of contiguous counties. Counties may join, leave, or form new states per Article IV §3.

### Section 2: State Powers (Delegated by Counties)

States exercise **only** these powers, delegated by member counties via interstate compact:

- State Guard (defense only, no federalization)
- Appellate courts (review of county trial courts)
- Interstate compacts (water, rail, cyber, criminal investigation)
- Land title registry (statewide recording)
- Sovereign wealth fund (severance tax, state land revenue)
- Coordination of county mutual aid (reinsurance, portability)
- **Minimum standards** in five spillover domains only: (a) air and water pollution that crosses a county line; (b) building, fire, and seismic safety; (c) election administration, ballot audit, and records interoperability; (d) court procedure and case-record interoperability; (e) public-health surveillance and reporting to the state compact.
- **Charter regional entities** (banks, mutuals, utilities, and compact operating companies) that operate in two or more counties, with opt-in by each participating county commission. Single-county entities remain county-chartered.

### Section 2.1: State Minimum Standards (Floors, Not Ceilings)

1. **Closed list.** The state may set minimum standards only in the five domains enumerated in §2. Adding a domain requires an amendment under Art V (34 states to call a convention, 38 to ratify). The Council of Counties cannot add a domain by statute.
2. **Floor, not ceiling.** A county may adopt a stricter standard. A state standard does not preempt a stricter county standard.
3. **Funded or void.** A standard that imposes a net new cost on a county is void unless the state funds it. The state may not require county spending (public-finance.md §6). The state satisfies this either by reimbursing certified county costs or by providing the function directly. Mechanics:

   - **Certification.** Net new cost is certified annually by each affected county's treasurer: the incremental compliance cost the county would not have incurred absent the standard, net of savings. One-time costs are amortized over the standard's 5-year sunset. Certification is subject to the sortition audit (public-finance.md §9); disputes go to the State Court of Appeals (Art II §5).
   - **Pay-as-you-go adoption.** A standard may be adopted or re-adopted only if the same budget act appropriates its aggregate certified cost for the coming year. No carryover (public-finance.md §3).
   - **Seniority on shortfall.** If aggregate certified costs exceed available revenue, standards are funded in order of adoption (oldest first). A standard that cannot be funded lapses for that year and must be re-adopted — restarting the 90-day County Veto window (Art III §2).
   - **Self-executing nonpayment.** If the state fails to reimburse a certified claim within 90 days, the standard is void in that county for the following year.

4. **Charter opt-out.** A county charter may opt out of a minimum standard's internal application by 60% referendum, subject to paragraph 7. The opt-out is recorded with the state and reviewed every 5 years.
5. **Sunset.** Every minimum standard expires in 5 years unless re-adopted by the Council of Counties. Re-adoption is a new enactment for purposes of Art III §2; the 90-day County Veto window restarts. Lapse triggers the savings clause (paragraph 8).
6. **Vetoable.** Minimum standards are state acts and are subject to County Veto under Art III §2 (state-act window).
7. **No opt-out for cross-county harm.** A county may opt out of a minimum standard's internal application, but the opt-out does not shield the county from liability for cross-county harm caused by its conduct. A downstream county or affected resident may sue in the State Court of Appeals for injunctive relief and damages. Judgments are enforced under the inter-county enforcement compact (civil-liberties.md §3.6), extended to cover Art II §2.1 standards.
8. **Savings clause.** Lapse, charter opt-out, or voidance does not erase accrued rights or liabilities:

   - **Pending matters continue.** Civil claims and prosecutions for conduct occurring while the standard applied in that county proceed to conclusion under the standard as it then existed.
   - **Judgments survive.** Final judgments — damages and injunctions alike — are enforced under full faith & credit (Art IV §1). An injunction may be modified or dissolved by the issuing court on motion of any party; lapse or opt-out is grounds for modification, not automatic dissolution.
   - **No new enforcement.** After lapse, opt-out, or voidance, the standard supports no new enforcement in that county for later conduct.
   - **Common-law floor.** Post-lapse cross-county harm remains actionable under county common law — trespass, nuisance, strict liability — with State Court of Appeals jurisdiction as an inter-county dispute (Art II §5). Lapse lowers the floor to the common-law baseline; it does not open a liability vacuum.
   - **Arrears.** Certified costs incurred while the standard was in force remain state liabilities, paid with seniority over new obligations.

### Section 3: State Legislature

- **Council of Counties**: One delegate per county, appointed by county commission, recallable at will.
- **Simple majority** for compact administration and minimum standards. **Two-thirds** for new assessments. **Unanimous consent** of participating county commissions for any assessment funded by the infrastructure surcharge. **Unanimous consent** of the Council only for amendments to this Article.
- **Fiscal cap**: County contributions may not exceed 15% of county LVT revenue. An increase requires 2/3 of county commissions (population-weighted) to veto. The cap is a constitutional limit, not a statute; it survives the Year 5 sunset.
- **No general legislative authority**. Cannot tax, regulate, or police directly. Minimum standards under §2.1 are the sole exception, and they are floors, not ceilings.

### Section 4: State Executive

- **Governor**: Elected by Council of Counties (majority vote). 2 terms max (4 years each). Recallable by Council majority.
- **Adjutant General**: Appointed by Governor, confirmed by Council. Commands State Guard.
- **Attorney General**: Elected by joint vote of county commissions (one vote per county, majority), 4-year term, 2 terms max, recallable by county commissions (majority). Represents the state in compact litigation and advises the Council.
- **Suspensive veto**: The Governor may return any Council act within 10 days with written reasons. The Council may override by 2/3.
- **Coordination Emergency**: The Governor may declare a coordination emergency for 30 days, renewable once by Council majority. It is limited to cross-county disaster response, mutual-aid dispatch, and public-health coordination. The Governor may deploy the State Guard for up to 72 hours without county consent; beyond 72 hours requires consent of each affected county commission. The Governor may spend only from a pre-funded State Emergency Reserve capped at 2% of the prior year's state budget. **No rights suspension, no business or school closure, no requisition, no curfew.**
- **No pardon power** (commutation in guard courts-martial requires Council 2/3).

### Section 5: State Judiciary

- **State Court of Appeals**: 9 judges, appointed by Council, confirmed by sortition jury (3 per county). 12-year max terms. Sortition review every 4 years.
- **Jurisdiction**: Appeals from county trial courts on questions of state compact law, inter-county disputes, constitutional questions.
- **Original jurisdiction**: inter-county disputes where compact arbitration fails, compact interpretation, cross-county harm claims under Art II §2.1(7), and minimum-standard reimbursement-certification disputes under Art II §2.1(3).
- **Advisory opinions**: non-binding, to the Council of Counties, on the legality of proposed compact provisions.
- **Appeal as of right** for constitutional questions arising under a state compact; discretionary review otherwise.
- **No certiorari over county law generally.**

### Section 6: Statewide Prosecution (Narrow Lane)

The Attorney General may convene a Statewide Grand Jury and prosecute only:

1. multi-county organized crime;
2. public corruption spanning two or more counties;
3. cross-county environmental crimes under Art II §2.1 standards, including civil abatement of cross-county harm.

Authorization requires either (a) consent of the county district attorney or sheriff in each affected county, or (b) 2/3 vote of the Council of Counties. The Attorney General has **no general police power, no state police force, and no authority over county sheriffs**. Arrests, warrants, and service of process remain with the county sheriff. If the sheriff refuses to execute a warrant or court order in an authorized statewide case, the State Court of Appeals may appoint a special marshal from any qualified sheriff of another county — execution is a duty of the office, attached to the warrant or order, not a product of compact membership (civil-liberties.md §3.6). The refusing sheriff is subject to personal liability and recall. **If any sheriff refuses to execute a lawful order under Art VI, Art II §2.1, or Art II §6 for 72 hours, the State Court of Appeals shall automatically appoint a special marshal from any qualified sheriff of another county; the refusing sheriff forfeits bond, faces personal liability, and is subject to immediate recall.** The State Bureau of Investigation remains limited to compact-listed interstate crimes.

## Article III: The Confederation (Vestigial Shell)

### Section 1: Delegated Powers (Exhaustive)

The Confederation exercises **only** these powers:

1. **Declare war** (requires county referendum — Art III §4)
2. **Regulate interstate commerce** (narrow: prevent county trade barriers, standardize weights/measures, enforce contracts across county lines)
3. **Admit new states** (Art IV §3)
4. **Propose constitutional amendments** (Art V)
5. **Receive foreign diplomats** (Diplomatic Receipt Office)
6. **Mint coin** (gold, silver, copper — US Mint)
7. **Maintain nuclear deterrent** (Governors' Nuclear Council — unanimous launch consent; physical removal requires 2/3 of states).
8. **Coordinate defense interoperability** (IDCO — voluntary standards only)

### Section 2: Congress (Legislative Only)

- **Senate (Council of States)**: 2 delegates per state, appointed by state legislature, recallable at will. No terms.
- **House (County Delegates)**: Cube-root size (~690). Multi-member districts (STV) at county level. Virtual attendance. Recallable by county commission (2/3 vote). 3 terms max (2 years each).
- **Sessions**: 90 days/year max. Single-subject bills. No staff. No omnibus.
- **Passage**: Simple majority both chambers. **No presidential veto** (no president).
- **Nullification (federal acts)**: Any federal act voided by majority of county commissions representing >50% US population (County Veto). No deadline.
- **Nullification (state acts)**: Any state act voided by majority of county commissions representing >50% US population, within 90 days of enactment. After 90 days, the state act stands unless repealed by the Council. Re-adoption or amendment of a state act is a new enactment; the 90-day window restarts.

### Section 3: Supreme Court (Judicial Only)

- **Jurisdiction**: Only federal statutes vs. this Constitution. **No certiorari over state/county law**.
- **Selection**: 9 justices drawn by lot from pool of 180 (3 per state, nominated by state legislatures, **confirmed by sortition jury** of citizens).
- **Terms**: 18 years, staggered. No life tenure.
- **Enforcement**: None. Relies on voluntary compliance. **Interposition**: 20+ state legislatures may nullify a ruling for their jurisdictions.
- **Jury nullification**: Mandatory instruction in all federal cases.

### Section 4: War Powers

Offensive military action requires **ALL**:

1. Congressional Declaration of War (simple majority, recorded vote)
2. County referendum: majority of counties nationally (population-weighted) approve within 90 days
3. State opt-in: each state legislature consents to Guard deployment
4. County opt-in: each county commission consents to militia deployment
5. Funding: special war tax levied **only on consenting counties**, collected by county treasurers
6. Duration: 1 year max. Renewal requires new referendum.

### Section 5: Executive Branch — **Abolished**

No President. No Cabinet. No agencies. No executive orders. No emergency powers. No commander-in-chief. No pardon power.

### Section 6: Vestigial Offices

1. **Diplomatic Receipt Office** (12 staff): Accept credentials, maintain treaty registry. No outgoing diplomacy.
2. **United States Mint** (200 staff): Strike coin per Art III §1(6). No paper, no digital dollar, no debt.
3. **Interstate Defense Coordination Office** (12 staff): Secure comms between state adjutants general. Voluntary standards.
4. **Governors' Nuclear Council** (virtual): Encrypted link between 50 governors for unanimous launch consent. **Surviving-majority fallback with proof of life**: Following a confirmed nuclear strike, governors must return a cryptographic proof-of-life ping within a strict time window (default 30 minutes). Governors who fail to respond are presumed unreachable. The denominator shifts from 50 to the number of governors confirmed alive and reachable. A two-thirds majority of those confirmed alive and reachable may authorize launch. The Council maintains a verified-alive registry updated quarterly and a cryptographic proof-of-life protocol tested monthly.

**Total: 224 staff** (Diplomatic Receipt 12 + Mint 200 + IDCO 12 + Council 0). The Council is virtual — no staff.

## Article IV: Inter-County / Interstate Relations

### Section 1: Full Faith & Credit

County public acts, records, judicial proceedings recognized across counties. Judgments enforceable via county sheriff.

### Section 2: Privileges & Immunities

Citizens of each county enjoy equal privileges in all counties. No county may discriminate against non-residents in fundamental rights (travel, contract, property, courts).

### Section 3: New States / Secession

- **New state**: Contiguous counties (min. 3) petition Congress. Admitted by simple majority both chambers.
- **Secession**: Contiguous counties (any number) may secede to form new state by 60% referendum in each county. Federal property bought at assessed value. Confederation debts allocated per capita.
- **County exit from state**: County may leave its state by 60% referendum + acceptance by new state's Council of Counties.
- **Automatic consolidation offer**: A county with population under 10,000, or one unable to staff a sheriff, court, and commission for 2 consecutive years, receives an automatic consolidation offer from the State Council — the largest contiguous county within the same state. Consolidation requires consent of both county commissions and a majority referendum in each (Art I §4). Contiguous small counties may also jointly consolidate into a single county, with commission consent and a single majority referendum across all participating counties. Voluntary; no merger is forced.

### Section 4: Compacts

Counties/states may form compacts for any shared purpose. Compacts are contracts: voluntary, specific, terminable by any party per its terms. No compact may create a new level of government.

### Section 5: Territories & District of Columbia

- **Territories** (Puerto Rico, Guam, USVI, American Samoa, CNMI): Binding referendum on statehood, independence, or free association within 2 years of ratification. No permanent territorial status. No colonial relationship.
- **District of Columbia**: Binding referendum on statehood, retrocession to Maryland, or independent city-state within 2 years. No federal district.
- **Transition**: Federal assets in territories/DC transferred to the new polity. Residents retain all Art VI rights throughout.

### Section 6: Tribal Sovereignty

- **Tribal trust lands** (56M acres) transferred to recognized tribes as sovereign title. Inalienable without tribal consent.
- **Treaty rights** (hunting, fishing, water, sovereignty) honored as binding.
- **Tribes** may choose: (a) sovereign nation status (COFA-style relationship), or (b) county status within a state (if tribe consents).
- **Federal trust responsibility** transferred to state compact or direct tribal-Confederation relationship (minimal).
- **No termination**. No allotment. No diminishment without tribal consent.

### Section 7: Legacy Debts & Obligations

- The Confederation **inherits no debt** of the prior federal government.
- All legacy claims (public debt, trust funds, pensions, contracts, contingent guarantees) are settled once by the **Transition Trust** per `debt-obligations.md`.
- Settlement is funded by federal asset sales, seigniorage, and a 5-year transition levy. No perpetual liability.
- After Year 10, the Transition Trust dissolves. Residual assets → counties per capita.
- **No successor liability**. The Confederation's budget (<$60M/yr) cannot be attached for legacy claims.

## Article V: Amendment

### Section 1: Proposal

Amendments proposed by:

- **Congress** (2/3 both chambers) — _rare, requires County Veto survival_
- **State legislatures** (34 states call Convention) — _primary path_

### Section 2: Ratification

Ratified by **38 state legislatures** (or state conventions). No Congress role. No presidential signature. No time limit.

### Section 3: Entrenched Provisions (Cannot Be Amended)

- Article I (County Sovereignty)
- Article III §5 (No Executive Branch)
- Article III §4 (War Powers — unanimous consent chain)

## Article VI: Rights Retained by the People

### Section 1: Incorporation

The Bill of Rights (Amendments I–VIII, US Constitution 1791) applies **fully and directly** to all counties, states, and the Confederation. No "incorporation doctrine" — rights are original, not derivative.

### Section 2: Enumerated Rights (Non-Exhaustive)

1. **Speech, press, assembly, petition** — no licensing, no prior restraint, no "free speech zones."
2. **Arms** — open/concealed carry without permit. No registration. No bans on classes of arms. County may regulate _negligent discharge_ only.
3. **Religion** — no establishment, no prohibition. No tax funding of religion.
4. **Due process** — life, liberty, property. Jury trial in all criminal cases and civil cases >$25,000 (7 jurors). Small claims (≤$25,000) are decided by a resident panel (3–5 randomly selected residents), which satisfies the jury guarantee. Grand jury for infamous crimes.
5. **Search/seizure** — warrants required (county judge, particularized). No bulk surveillance. No civil asset forfeiture.
6. **Self-incrimination** — silence absolute. No compelled decryption.
7. **Cruel/unusual punishment** — no death penalty. No solitary >15 days. Restorative justice preferred.
8. **Equal protection** — no classification by race, sex, religion, origin. Strict scrutiny.
9. **Privacy** — papers, effects, communications, genetic data, location. Warrant required.
10. **Travel** — right to move, reside, work across county/state lines. No internal passports.
11. **Contract** — freedom to associate, exchange, organize. No forced arbitration. No adhesion waivers.
12. **Property** — just compensation for taking. Regulatory taking = taking. LVT ≠ taking.
13. **Asylum & non-refoulement** — no person within a county's jurisdiction may be returned to a country where they face credible fear of persecution. The county must parole such a person pending a hearing (border-immigration.md). This right limits county sovereignty under Art I §2.

### Section 3: Remedies

- **Jury nullification**: Mandatory instruction. Jurors judge law + fact.
- **Sortition audit**: Random citizens review government action quarterly.
- **Personal liability**: Officials personally liable for rights violations. No qualified immunity. County indemnifies only if actor followed county policy.
- **County veto**: Nullifies any state/federal rights infringement.
- **Cross-county execution (duty of office)**: Sheriffs execute lawful orders from courts of competent jurisdiction — including cross-county orders enforcing an Art VI right that a non-compliant county refuses. The duty derives from the office plus the court order; the inter-county enforcement compact coordinates rosters, procedures, and reimbursement (civil-liberties.md §3.6).
- **Interposition**: 20+ states nullify federal rights violation.
- **Secession**: Ultimate exit.

## Article VII: Supremacy & Hierarchy

### Section 1: Hierarchy of Law

1. **This Constitution**
2. **County charters** (ratified by residents)
3. **State compacts** (ratified by county commissions)
4. **State minimum standards** (Art II §2.1)
5. **Confederation statutes** (passed by Congress, surviving County Veto)
6. **County ordinances**
7. **State compact regulations**
8. **Confederation regulations** (IDCO standards, Mint specs — voluntary)

### Section 2: Conflict Resolution

- County charter vs. state compact → **County charter prevails** (counties delegate up, not down).
- County charter vs. state minimum standard → **County charter prevails only if it contains a recorded 60% opt-out; otherwise the standard is a floor.**
- State compact vs. state minimum standard → **the stricter standard governs.**
- State compact vs. Confederation statute → **State compact prevails** (states delegate up, not down).
- All conflicts resolved by **county trial court** → **state appellate court** → **SCOTUS** (only if Confederation statute vs. Constitution).

### Section 3: No Implied Powers

**Expressio unius est exclusio alterius**. Every delegation is narrow, specific, and strictly construed. No "necessary and proper" clause. No "general welfare" clause. No "commerce clause" expansion.

## Article VIII: Transition & Ratification

### Section 1: Ratification

The **Decree** (Year 0) dissolves the prior federal order and **promulgates** this Constitution for ratification. The Constitution takes effect **provisionally** from the Decree (to enable the transition) and becomes **permanently binding** when ratified by county referendums in **2,000+ counties** representing >50% US population (target: Year 2). If ratification fails by Year 3, the Decree lapses, interim governance dissolves, and a new constitutional convention is called.

### Section 2: Transition (5 Years)

- **Year 0**: Dictatorial decree **dissolves the prior federal order and promulgates Articles I–VII for ratification**. Federal executive abolished. Fed abolished. Income tax abolished. Mint strikes coin.
- **Year 1**: Counties adopt charters (including the Art II §2.1 opt-out procedure). LVT + consumption tax implemented. Mutuals chartered. State compacts formed. County commissions seated; joint vote elects the State Attorney General. Council of Counties seated; elects the Governor. Governor's suspensive veto and coordination-emergency rules take effect. Council adopts the first state minimum standards under the pay-as-you-go rule (Art II §2.1(3)). State may charter regional entities with county opt-in.
- **Year 2**: Federal budget <$100M. Congress 90-day session. State guards operational. County militias training. State Courts of Appeals seated; original jurisdiction and advisory-opinion rules take effect. First state-act County Veto test (90-day window). State infrastructure surcharge may be levied on participating counties only (1% during Years 2–5, 2% after the Trust levy sunsets; 5-year sunset); State Emergency Reserve funded (≤2% prior-year budget). Minimum-standard reimbursement appropriated in the state budget (Art II §2.1(3) pay-as-you-go).
- **Year 3**: Competitive currency normal. Interstate compacts active. SCOTUS sortition seated. Interposition threshold (20 states) tested. State AG statewide grand jury rules operational.
- **Year 4**: System stable. Federal shell <$60M. SWF dividends flowing. State minimum standards reviewed for cost and efficacy.
- **Year 5**: **All prior federal/state statutes sunset**. Only re-authorized items survive. County charters re-ratified. State minimum standards re-adopted or lapse (re-adoption may not add a domain; that requires Art V). Re-adoption restarts the 90-day County Veto window; lapse triggers the Art II §2.1(8) savings clause. Transition complete.
- **Exception**: The **Transition Trust** (Art IV §7) continues through **Year 10** to settle legacy claims, then dissolves. It is a wind-down vehicle, not a program subject to the Year 5 sunset. Its instruments are time-bounded (≤10 years) and its county assessment sunsets with it.

### Section 3: The Vanish

The dictator publishes all decrees, code, data. Appoints county stewards by sortition (one per county, 1-year term). Dissolves federal executive. Walks away. **No successor. No center. No ruler.**

## Article IX: Interpretation

### Section 1: Strict Construction

This Constitution shall be interpreted **narrowly against power, broadly toward liberty**. Ambiguities resolved in favor of county sovereignty and individual rights.

### Section 2: No Judicial Supremacy

No court has final interpretive authority. The people, through counties, states, and interposition, are the final interpreters.

### Section 3: Sunset Review

Every provision of this Constitution (except Art V §3) expires in **50 years** unless re-ratified by the Article V process. Forces each generation to consent.
