# Civil Liberties Enforcement System

**Design Principle**: _Rights without remedies are suggestions. Every right in Article VI Constitution has a specific, accessible, county-level enforcement mechanism. No "qualified immunity." No exhaustion of federal remedies. No standing barriers._

---

## 1. Enforcement Architecture (Layered)

| Layer                 | Mechanism                                                | Trigger                | Forum                                            | Timeline          |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------- |
| **1. Self-Help**      | Jury nullification, encryption, exit, non-compliance     | Immediate              | N/A                                              | Instant           |
| **2. Administrative** | Sheriff refusal, clerk refusal, commission nullification | Official action        | County office                                    | Days              |
| **3. Judicial**       | Injunction, damages, suppression, habeas, mandamus       | Rights violation       | County trial court                               | 24 hrs – 180 days |
| **4. Structural**     | County veto, sortition audit, interposition, secession   | Systemic violation     | County commission / State legislature / Counties | 30 days – 2 years |
| **5. Ultimate**       | Secession (Art IV §3)                                    | Irreconcilable tyranny | County referendum (60%)                          | 1–3 years         |

**No federal court access required.** All remedies originate and terminate at county level (except interposition/secession).

## 2. Specific Rights → Specific Remedies

### Article VI §2.1: Speech, Press, Assembly, Petition

| Violation                                       | Primary Remedy                                  | Secondary                       | Tertiary                        |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Prior restraint** (permit denial, injunction) | Emergency injunction (county judge, 24 hrs)     | Damages ($10k/day)              | Sheriff refuses to enforce      |
| **Content-based restriction**                   | Facial challenge → void ab initio               | Attorney fees + costs           | County veto nullifies ordinance |
| **Retaliation** (firing, licensing, audit)      | Reinstatement + back pay + damages              | Personal liability for official | Sortition audit → recall        |
| **Surveillance of speech**                      | Suppression + statutory damages ($50k/incident) | Equipment destruction order     | Criminal referral (official)    |
| **Assembly dispersal**                          | Injunction + damages                            | Sheriff personal liability      | Commission policy change        |

**No "free speech zones." No permit for <50 people. No content-neutral time/place/manner without narrow tailoring + ample alternatives.**

### Article VI §2.2: Arms

| Violation                        | Primary Remedy                  | Secondary              | Tertiary                      |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Carry ban** (open/concealed)   | Emergency injunction (24 hrs)   | Damages ($25k + fees)  | Sheriff refuses to enforce    |
| **Registration/licensing**       | Facial challenge → void         | Damages ($10k + fees)  | County veto nullifies         |
| **Class ban** (AR-15, mags, NFA) | Facial challenge → void         | Damages ($50k + fees)  | Interposition (20 states)     |
| **Red flag seizure**             | Habeas corpus (48 hrs) + return | Damages ($100k + fees) | Sheriff refuses seizure order |
| **Prohibited person expansion**  | Strict scrutiny challenge       | Damages                | Sortition audit of list       |

**No background checks for private sales. No waiting periods. No "sensitive places" beyond courthouses/jails. Negligent discharge = only regulable offense.**

### Article VI §2.3: Religion

| Violation                                | Primary Remedy             | Secondary                   | Tertiary      |

## 3. Structural Remedies (Systemic Enforcement)

### 3.1 County Veto (Art III §2)

- **Trigger**: Any state/federal law, regulation, or action violating rights.
- **Process (federal acts)**: Majority of county commissions representing >50% US population vote to nullify. No deadline.
- **Process (state acts)**: Majority of county commissions representing >50% US population vote to nullify within 90 days of enactment. After 90 days, the state act stands unless repealed by the Council. **Re-adoption or amendment of a state act is a new enactment — the 90-day window restarts** (Art III §2), so re-adopted minimum standards are re-vetoable.
- **Effect**: Nullified _ab initio_ in those counties. No enforcement. No penalties for non-compliance.
- **Frequency**: No limit. No cooling-off. Immediate.
- **Enforcement**: Sheriffs refuse to cooperate. Clerks refuse to record. Courts dismiss charges.

### 3.2 Sortition Audit (Quarterly)

- **Selection**: 25 residents per county (stratified by age, sex, race, income). 1-year term, no repeat for 5 years.
- **Mandate**: Audit government action for rights violations. Subpoena power. Public hearings. Public report.
- **Scope**: Sheriff, DA, judges, commission, clerk, health mutual, pension trust, schools, contractors.
- **Output**: "Rights Report Card" (A–F). Published. Triggers recall if F.
- **Funding**: 0.1% LVT (mandatory). Independent of audited entities.

### 3.3 Personal Liability (No Qualified Immunity)

| Official                  | Bond                                 | Liability Standard                     | Indemnification                            |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Sheriff/Deputies**      | 10% county LVT (min $50k, max $500k) | Knowingly/recklessly violate rights    | ONLY if policy followed + training current |
| **Prosecutors**           | $250k                                | Brady violation, malicious prosecution | ONLY if good faith + supervisor approved   |
| **Judges**                | $250k                                | Malicious/knowing rights violation     | ONLY if good faith legal error             |
| **Commissioners**         | $100k                                | Legislative act violating rights       | ONLY if relied on counsel + public hearing |
| **Clerks/Administrators** | $50k                                 | Ministerial refusal of rights          | ONLY if followed policy                    |
| **SBI Agents**            | $500k                                | Same as deputies                       | ONLY if compact procedure followed         |

**Bond paid by official (or insurance). County pays premium ONLY for good-faith errors. Judgments paid from bond → personal assets → insurance. Taxpayers never pay.**

### 3.4 Interposition (Art III §3, Art VI §3)

- **Trigger**: SCOTUS ruling violating rights, or federal statute/regulation violating rights.
- **Process**: 20+ state legislatures pass concurrent resolution nullifying for their jurisdictions.
- **Effect**: Federal law/ruling unenforceable in those states. No federal penalty.
- **Escalation**: Forces constitutional amendment (Art V) or Convention.
- **History**: Used 3x in first 10 years (surveillance, gun ban, emergency powers). Each forced amendment or repeal.

### 3.5 Secession (Art IV §3, Art VI §3)

- **Trigger**: Irreconcilable rights violations. No other remedy effective.
- **Process**: Contiguous counties (any number) → 60% referendum each → form new state → admitted by Congress (simple majority).
- **Federal property**: Bought at assessed value (county assessor).
- **Debt allocation**: Per capita.
- **Rights guarantee**: New state charter must incorporate Art VI or be denied admission.
- **Used**: 2x in first 20 years (surveillance state, mandatory service). Both admitted as states within 18 months.

### 3.6 Inter-County Enforcement Compact (Rights + Minimum Standards)

**The gap it closes**: The Confederation has no police or executive power. If a county's own sheriff refuses to honor an Art VI right (e.g., asylum parole under Art VI §2.13) or a State Court of Appeals order under Art II §2.1 (cross-county harm) or Art II §6 (AG prosecution), the county's own courts and recall may be captured by the same majority. Other sheriffs execute the order as a **duty of their office**; the compact coordinates the effort — mutual aid in form, duty in law, never a central police force.

- **Trigger**: A county refuses to comply with a state court order or a sortition audit finding on an Art VI right, **or** refuses to enforce a State Court of Appeals order under Art II §2.1 (cross-county harm) or Art II §6 (AG prosecution).
- **Mechanism**: Execution authority derives from **the office plus the court order** — not from compact membership. The compact maintains cross-deputization rosters and procedures. A team (from 2+ counties) may enter the non-compliant county to enforce the specific right, standard, or order (e.g., escort an asylum seeker to a sponsoring county, serve the court order or warrant, secure evidence, abate the cross-county discharge).
- **Governance**: Compact board (1 per member county). Activation requires **2/3 of members** for compact-coordinated joint teams. A court-appointed marshal or team acts on the court order alone — no board vote required (execution is a duty of office).
- **Limits**: No lethal force. No occupation. Only the specific right, standard, or prosecution at issue. Withdraws when compliance is achieved. County sheriff of the non-compliant county retains authority over all other matters.
- **No state police**: This compact is the enforcement backstop for opt-out counties and AG-authorized prosecutions. It does not create a state police force.
- **Surviving orders**: Standard lapse or county opt-out does not dissolve orders issued under Art II §2.1; the compact keeps enforcing surviving orders (Art II §2.1(8) savings clause).
- **Funding**: Member county assessments (per capita).
- **Opt-out**: A county may decline compact **coordination** (rosters, funding) — but cannot opt out of its own sheriff's duty to execute lawful orders, nor of other sheriffs executing orders against it for Art VI rights or surviving orders. A validly opted-out standard supports no new enforcement (Art II §2.1(8)). A county that leaves also loses coordinated protection for its own residents if _it_ is the target of another county's violation.
- **Economic Containment (Primary Escalation)**: Before any physical marshal dispatch, member counties may impose **economic containment** at their own borders — no central authority required. Each county independently may: suspend the non-compliant county's CRCs at their borders; freeze the non-compliant county's access to inter-county clearinghouses; sever regional infrastructure compacts (water, power, transit, rail) at their border; halt road maintenance and transit at county lines; restrict cross-county mutual aid deployments. These are **internal border actions**, not armed invasion — each county exercises its sovereign right to control its borders and compact participation. Economic containment makes refusal expensive immediately, without armed escalation.

- **Compact Expulsion (Secondary Escalation)**: If economic containment fails, the compact board may vote (2/3 majority) to expel the non-compliant county from the compact. Expulsion terminates all compact benefits (water, power, transit, mutual aid, clearinghouse access) and triggers the secession timeline (Art IV §3). Expulsion is a compact decision, not a State Council mandate — voluntary coordination, not central enforcement.

- **Residual limit (intentional)**: A county apparatus that physically resists a cross-deputized team can do so until the political backstops move — the design has no occupation force (no lethal force, no occupation). The terminal remedies are the refusing sheriff's personal liability and recall, the county veto, interposition, and secession. Compliance is made cheap and refusal expensive, but never compelled at gunpoint.
- **Precedent**: Modeled on interstate mutual aid compacts (wildfire, hurricane) and the Criminal Investigation Compact (law-enforcement.md).

### 4.2 State Civil Rights Commission (Per State)

- **Composition**: 1 commissioner per county (appointed by county commission).

- **Composition**: 1 rep per state (appointed by state legislature).
- **Role**: Maintain treaty registry (human rights treaties). Receive foreign complaints. **No enforcement.**
- **Staff**: 3 people (part of Diplomatic Receipt Office).

## 5. Litigation Access (No Barriers)

### 5.1 Standing

- **Any resident** may sue for rights violation (no "injury in fact" beyond violation).
- **Any organization** may sue on behalf of members.
- **Any county** may sue state/federal for violation of its residents' rights.
- **No taxpayer standing barrier** — rights violation = injury.

### 5.2 Fees & Costs

- **No filing fees** for rights cases.
- **Attorney fees**: Prevailing plaintiff = fees + costs (mandatory). Prevailing defendant = fees only if frivolous (judge certifies).
- **Contingency**: 33% cap (rights cases). Court approves.
- **Legal Aid Trust**: Funded by 1% court fees + donations. Provides navigators + attorneys for indigent.

### 5.3 Procedure

- **Emergency docket**: Rights cases heard in 24 hrs (injunctions, habeas).
- **Discovery**: Automatic, mandatory, broad. Sanctions for games.
- **Jury**: 7 jurors (rights cases). Nullification instruction mandatory.
- **Appeal**: Only to State Court of Appeals (compact law) or SCOTUS (federal statute vs. Constitution). No intermediate appeal.

### 5.4 Remedies

| Remedy                   | Standard                                    | Cap                   |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| **Compensatory damages** | Actual harm (economic + non-economic)       | None                  |
| **Statutory damages**    | Per violation (see §2 table)                | None                  |
| **Punitive damages**     | Malice/recklessness                         | 3x compensatory       |
| **Injunctive relief**    | Broad (policy change, training, monitoring) | None                  |
| **Declaratory relief**   | Binding on all similarly situated           | None                  |
| **Attorney fees**        | Prevailing plaintiff (mandatory)            | Reasonable (lodestar) |
| **Costs**                | Prevailing party                            | Actual                |

## 11. Edge Cases & Answers

| Question                                     | Answer                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **County refuses to enforce rights?**        | Sortition audit → recall → personal liability → County Veto (other counties) → Interposition → Secession.                                |
| **Sheriff ignores injunction?**              | Contempt → daily fines ($10k) from bond → removal by Civil Service Board → recall.                                                       |
| **DA refuses to prosecute official?**        | Civil Rights Attorney prosecutes (private attorney general). Sortition grand jury indicts.                                               |
| **Judge refuses nullification instruction?** | Sortition audit → recall → personal liability → judicial discipline (Civil Service Board).                                               |
| **State compact violates rights?**           | County Veto nullifies in that county. Interposition if widespread.                                                                       |
| **Federal government ignores County Veto?**  | No enforcement mechanism needed — sheriffs don't cooperate, clerks don't record, courts dismiss. Federal shell has no muscle.            |
| **Rights conflict (speech vs. privacy)?**    | County trial court balances (strict scrutiny for both). Appeal → State Appeals (compact law) or SCOTUS (federal statute).                |
| **Emergency suspension of rights?**          | **No emergency exception** in Constitution. Art III §4 war powers only. Pandemic/asteroid = county civil defense (no rights suspension). |
| **Private actor violates rights?**           | County law applies (public accommodation, employment, housing). County court hears. No state action requirement.                         |

**The system is done when**: A resident in any county, with no lawyer, no money, no connections, can walk into their county courthouse, file a one-page form, get a hearing in 24 hours, face a jury of their neighbors, win damages paid from the official's personal bond, and see the policy changed — all without leaving their county. And if the county refuses, 2,000 other counties back them up. And if the state refuses, 20 states nullify. And if the Confederation refuses, they leave.

**Rights are not granted. They are enforced. Here, enforcement is local, personal, and unavoidable.**
