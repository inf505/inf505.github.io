# Debt, Obligations & Legacy Liabilities

**Purpose:** Resolve every financial claim the old federal state leaves behind — debt, pensions, entitlements, contracts — without creating a successor government that can be held liable for them.

**Core principle:** _The Confederation inherits no debt and assumes no obligation. Claims are settled once, at transition, by explicit rule. No bailouts, no inflation, no perpetual liability._

**No successor can carry this.** The settlement must be decisive.

## 2. Guiding Rules (The Five Settlement Principles)

1. **No repudiation of private holders without compensation.** Marketable Treasuries are paid at face value in coin or asset transfer. The Confederation does not default on private creditors.
2. **No assumption of intragovernmental IOUs.** Trust fund "assets" are accounting entries, not claims on future taxpayers. They are extinguished, and beneficiaries are transitioned to county mutuals.
3. **No unfunded pension transferred.** Accrued benefits are honored via funded trusts; future accrual stops at transition.
4. **No contingent guarantee survives.** Deposit insurance, mortgage guarantees, and pension guarantees are wound down or privatized.
5. **No perpetual liability.** Every settlement is one-time, time-bounded, and funded from asset sales + seigniorage + a temporary transition levy.

## 3. Public Debt Held by the Public (~$27T)

### 3.1 Settlement Mechanism

| Step               | Action                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Freeze**      | On Decree Day, all Treasury issuance stops. Existing securities remain valid claims.                                                                                                   |
| **2. Verify**      | Holders register claims with the Transition Trust (digital + paper).                                                                                                                   |
| **3. Pay**         | Claims paid at face value + accrued interest, in one of: (a) coin, (b) federal asset transfer (land, buildings, spectrum), (c) Transition Trust notes (5-year, backed by asset sales). |
| **4. No rollover** | No new federal debt issued. Maturity ladder honored from Trust assets.                                                                                                                 |

### 3.2 Funding the Payoff

| Source                 | Est. Value    | Notes                                            |
| ---------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Federal land sales** | ~$2–5T        | BLM/USFS surplus parcels, not parks/wilderness   |
| **Federal buildings**  | ~$500B        | DC, regional offices                             |
| **Spectrum auctions**  | ~$500B        | FCC-held licenses                                |
| **Strategic reserves** | ~$100B        | SPR, helium, minerals                            |
| **Gold revaluation**   | ~$500B        | Treasury gold at market                          |
| **Seigniorage (coin)** | ~$50B/yr      | Mint profit during transition                    |
| **Transition levy**    | ~$500B/yr × 5 | Temporary, on high-income/high-wealth (see §3.3) |
| **Asset sales total**  | **~$4–7T**    | Covers ~20–25% of debt                           |

### 3.3 The Gap (Honest Accounting)

Asset sales + levy cover ~$5–8T of $27T. The remainder (~$19–22T) must be handled by one of:

- **Haircut**: Negotiated reduction with holders (e.g., 50 cents on the dollar). Requires creditor consent or collective action clause.
- **10-year Transition Trust notes**: Transition Trust instruments paying a 1% coupon, funded by a **temporary** county assessment that **sunsets with the Trust at Year 10**. Terminology note: these are **not consols** — a consol is a perpetuity, and these self-liquidate at Year 10. Not government debt.
- **Currency conversion**: Pay in coin at a revalued rate (inflationary for holders, but no default).

**Recommended**: **Haircut + 10-year Trust notes**. Private holders take a 40–50% haircut (recognizing the old regime's insolvency); remaining claims converted to 1% Trust notes funded by a capped county assessment (~0.5% of LVT) that **ends when the Trust dissolves at Year 10**. This is a default in substance but negotiated and transparent.

**Alternative (harder line)**: Full repudiation of debt held by foreign governments and the Fed (the largest holders), pay domestic retail holders in full. Politically explosive; legally cleaner.

### 3.4 Voluntary Conversion Premium (Equity-Driven Transition Levy)

Instead of a purely punitive wealth levy, high-income/high-wealth payers may **opt in** to a **Voluntary Conversion Premium**:

- **Mechanism**: Payer directs their transition levy payment (or a portion thereof) into newly chartered **county infrastructure mutuals** — grid, fiber, rail, ports, water systems — receiving **proportional equity stakes** in those mutuals.
- **Valuation**: Equity allocated at mutual charter valuation (independent appraisal). Levy payment = equity purchase at par.
- **Rights**: Equity carries standard mutual member rights (governance, dividends, capital appreciation). No special privileges.
- **Liquidity**: Equity tradable on county mutual secondary markets after Year 3.
- **Voluntary**: Payer chooses standard levy (pure tax) or Conversion Premium (equity). No coercion.
- **Effect**: Turns a punitive levy into a productive seed investment; aligns wealthy interests with local infrastructure success; anchors capital locally.

### 3.5 Phased Opt-In Debt Swap Program (Retail Holders)

Before imposing the haircut, **retail Treasury holders** (individuals, not institutions) may **opt in** to a **Phased Debt Swap**:

- **Eligibility**: Individual holders of marketable Treasuries (direct or via brokerage), up to $500k face value per household.
- **Swap Options** (holder chooses one):
  1. **County LVT Credits**: Face value swapped for pre-paid LVT credits at participating counties (redeemable against future LVT obligations, transferable).
  2. **County TANs**: Face value swapped for County Tax Anticipation Notes (90–180 day, rolling, accepted for local tax payment, circulate as local liquidity).
  3. **Transition Trust Notes**: Face value swapped for 10-year Trust notes (1% coupon) at par, avoiding haircut.
- **Phasing**: Swap window opens Year 0, closes Year 2. After window closes, remaining retail holders subject to standard haircut.
- **Institutional Holders**: Not eligible — subject to standard haircut/Trust note conversion.
- **Effect**: Anchors domestic liquidity locally; creates immediate demand for county revenue instruments (LVT credits, TANs); retail holders avoid haircut; counties gain immediate capital for mutuals/infrastructure.

## 4. Intragovernmental Debt (~$7T) — Extinguished

### 4.1 The Accounting Fiction

Social Security and Medicare "trust funds" hold special-issue Treasuries — IOUs from one part of the government to another. They are not assets; they are claims on future tax revenue. **They are extinguished at transition.**

### 4.2 Beneficiary Transition

| Program             | Old Benefit         | New Arrangement                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Social Security** | Monthly check       | **County Pension Trust** (public-finance.md §5): accrued benefit honored at transition, funded by Transition Trust for current retirees; workers <55 transition to county DC plan. |
| **Medicare**        | Fee-for-service     | **County Health Mutual** (healthcare.md): all beneficiaries enrolled automatically. Premiums income-based.                                                                         |
| **Medicaid**        | State-federal match | **County Health Mutual** (same). No means test beyond premium subsidy.                                                                                                             |
| **SSI**             | Means-tested cash   | **County Disability Mutual** (domestic-policy.md §4).                                                                                                                              |
| **CHIP**            | Child coverage      | **County Health Mutual** (children automatic).                                                                                                                                     |

### 4.3 Funding the Transition (Current Beneficiaries)

| Group                     | Treatment                                                                      | Funding                           |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| **Retirees (65+)**        | Benefit honored at current level, paid by Transition Trust                     | Asset sales + levy (5 years)      |
| **Near-retirees (55–64)** | Benefit honored at 80% level, phased to county plan                            | Transition Trust + county pension |
| **Workers (<55)**         | Transition to county DC plan; accrued benefit preserved as lump-sum or annuity | County pension trust              |
| **Disabled**              | County disability mutual (no gap)                                              | County mutual + state reinsurance |

**No "trust fund" survives.** No intergenerational transfer beyond the 5-year transition.

- **VA hospitals/clinics** → County hospital trusts (asset transfer) or state compacts.
- **Veterans** = county health mutual members (automatic). Service-connected care = mutual covers, county LVT subsidizes.
- **No VA bureaucracy.** No "VA disability rating" for care access.

### 6.2 Disability Compensation

| Element                      | Settlement                                                                        |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

| **Clothing allowance, etc.** | Folded into disability payment                                                    |

### 6.3 Education / Home Loan / Insurance

- **GI Bill** → County education trust (tuition + stipend for veterans, 5 years post-transition).
- **VA home loan** → County mortgage mutual (see §8).

## 7. Contractor Claims & Procurement Backlog (~$500B)

### 7.1 Rules

| Claim Type                         | Treatment                                                              |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Completed work, unpaid invoice** | Paid in full from Transition Trust                                     |
| **In-progress contracts**          | Terminated for convenience; costs incurred + reasonable profit paid    |
| **Leases (federal offices)**       | Assumed by county/state if used; otherwise terminated with notice      |
| **Grants (research, non-profit)**  | Honored through transition year; no renewal                            |
| **Settlements/judgments**          | Paid from Transition Trust (capped at $1M/claim; larger → negotiation) |

### 7.2 No New Contracts

- **Decree Day**: All new federal contracting stops.
- **Existing**: Wound down per §7.1.
- **Disputes**: County trial court (not Court of Federal Claims).

## 8. Contingent Liabilities (Wound Down)

### 8.1 Deposit Insurance (FDIC/NCUA)

| Step                       | Action                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Freeze**              | No new deposits insured after Decree Day.                                                                                                          |
| **2. Wind down**           | Existing insured deposits remain covered for 2 years (Transition Trust backstop).                                                                  |
| **3. Privatize**           | County- or state-chartered deposit mutuals (private insurance) replace FDIC. Banks choose: mutual insurance or self-insure (capital requirements). |
| **4. No federal backstop** | After 2 years, no taxpayer guarantee.                                                                                                              |

### 8.2 Mortgage Guarantees (FHA/VA/Ginnie/Fannie/Freddie)

| Step             | Action                                                                         |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1. Freeze**    | No new guarantees after Decree Day.                                            |
| **2. Honor**     | Existing guarantees honored (Transition Trust) until loans mature or are sold. |
| **3. Privatize** | County- or state-chartered mortgage mutuals (private insurance) replace.       |
| **4. Wind down** | GSEs (Fannie/Freddie) sold to private investors or liquidated.                 |

### 8.3 Pension Guarantee (PBGC)

| Step             | Action                                                            |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Honor**     | Existing guaranteed benefits paid by Transition Trust.            |
| **2. Privatize** | County pension trusts (funded, DC) replace. No federal guarantee. |
| **3. Wind down** | PBGC dissolved after existing claims paid.                        |

### 8.4 Student Loans (~$1.7T)

| Step                  | Action                                                                                                          |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Freeze**         | No new federal loans after Decree Day.                                                                          |
| **2. Transfer**       | Portfolio sold to county/private lenders at discount (or held by Transition Trust).                             |
| **3. Discharge**      | Bankruptcy dischargeable (judicial-system.md). No means test.                                                   |
| **4. No forgiveness** | No blanket cancellation (moral hazard). But: income-based repayment capped at 10% for 10 years, then discharge. |

## 9. The Transition Trust (The Vehicle)

### 9.1 Structure

- **Legal form**: Independent trust, chartered by the Decree, governed by 7 sortition-selected trustees + 3 financial experts.
- **Mandate**: Pay legacy claims per this document. No other function.
- **Duration**: 10 years (5-year transition + 5-year tail). **Exempt from the Year 5 sunset** (constitution Art VIII §2; public-finance.md §10); hard end at Year 10.
- **Sunset**: Dissolves automatically at Year 10; residual assets → counties per capita. No perpetual liability.

### 9.2 Funding Sources

| Source                  | Est.         | Timing               |
| ----------------------- | ------------ | -------------------- |
| **Asset sales**         | $4–7T        | Years 0–5            |
| **Seigniorage**         | $250B        | Years 0–5            |
| **Transition levy**     | $2.5T        | Years 0–5            |
| **Haircut savings**     | $10–15T      | Year 0               |
| **Trust-note issuance** | $5–10T       | Years 0–10           |
| **Total**               | **~$22–35T** | Covers legacy claims |

### 9.3 The Transition Levy (Temporary)

| Base                        | Rate   | Duration |
| --------------------------- | ------ | -------- |
| **Wealth >$10M**            | 5%/yr  | 5 years  |
| **Income >$1M**             | 10%/yr | 5 years  |
| **Corporate assets >$100M** | 2%/yr  | 5 years  |
| **Financial transactions**  | 0.1%   | 5 years  |

**Sunset**: Automatic at Year 5. No extension without county referendum.

**Interaction with the state infrastructure surcharge**: The state surcharge (`public-finance.md` §3) is a carved-out share of the county-collected LVT base, not a separate state levy. It may not take effect before Year 2 and is capped at 1% during Years 2–5 while this levy is in force; it rises to 2% only after this levy sunsets at Year 5. The two levies do not share a base, but the sequencing keeps the total transition burden visible and bounded.

## 14. Edge Cases

| Question                                           | Answer                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Foreign holder refuses haircut?**                | Paid in coin at face value if they hold out (small holders). Large holders negotiate or litigate (no forum). |
| **Retiree dies during transition?**                | Survivor benefits honored. Estate claim paid.                                                                |
| **Contractor fraud?**                              | Claim denied. Referred to county DA.                                                                         |
| **State refuses to honor federal pension?**        | Transition Trust pays directly (not through state).                                                          |
| **County refuses to enroll Medicare beneficiary?** | Automatic enrollment (Constitution Art VI §2.4 due process).                                                 |
| **Hyperinflation risk from levy/seigniorage?**     | Levy is on wealth/income, not money printing. Seigniorage capped. Coin-based.                                |
| **What if asset sales fall short?**                | 10-year Trust notes issued (1% coupon). County assessment covers the coupon, sunsets with Trust at Year 10.  |
| **What if levy is evaded?**                        | County tax authorities enforce (LVT records, clearinghouse data).                                            |

## 15. The Honest Bottom Line

**The old regime is insolvent.** Its promises (~$60T+) exceed its assets (~$5T). No settlement can pay everyone in full without a successor state that taxes forever.

**This settlement:**

- Pays private creditors (mostly) to preserve the financial system.
- Extinguishes intragovernmental fiction (trust funds).
- Honors accrued pensions (transition cohort) but stops future accrual.
- Wounds down contingent guarantees (no perpetual backstop).
- Funds the gap with asset sales + a 5-year levy + 10-year Trust notes.
- **Leaves no successor liable.**

**The cost is borne by:** wealthy holders (levy), bondholders (haircut), future retirees (transition to DC), and the general public (5 years of reduced services + asset sales). **No one is made whole. Everyone knows the rules in advance.**

---

**The system is done when:** Every legacy claim is either paid, haircut, extinguished, or privatized — by Year 10 — and the Confederation's balance sheet is empty. No debt. No trust funds. No guarantees. No unfunded liabilities. **A clean slate, funded by the old regime's own assets, with the losses allocated by explicit rule rather than inflation or default-by-stealth.**
